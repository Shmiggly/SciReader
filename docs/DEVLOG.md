The miss was Planck's law: "divided by k sub B T" is ambiguous out loud, a perfect example of what verification is for. qwen3.5 described a synthetic XRD figure correctly. Learned: SymPy's LaTeX parser can't handle most physics notation (6/8 failed), so the verifier will compare canonical MathML trees instead; `ollama ps` under-reports gemma4's VRAM (~8.5 GB real), so the two LLMs must run in separate batches.
- Chose 5 sample papers (+1 backup) from the demo domain (polymers for additive manufacturing + ML), verified via OpenAlex and arXiv: licenses, LaTeX-source availability, equation and figure counts. Flagship: the CC BY physics-informed Bayesian learning paper on electrohydrodynamic polymer jet printing (33 equations). Learned: the arXiv search API rate-limited us immediately (OpenAlex is the fallback), arXiv licenses often differ from the CC BY journal version, and MDPI/Europe PMC block scripted PDF downloads.

## 2026-10-21 10:00–10:30 EDT: why equations fall back (diagnosis, first experiment)

- On the flagship, the narrator is not the main problem: the parser's formula OCR is. It turned K⁻¹ into K¹ in four equations (one of them then "verified": faithful to the parse, wrong about the page), τ_rr into τ_m, p into ρ, y into γ. The gate can only be as right as its source LaTeX.
- A local vision model reading each equation crop (qwen3.5:9b, under 2 s per equation) got most of these right and produced usable LaTeX for all three equations the parser garbled. It also makes its own mistakes, so the next step is two independent readings of the page, with the PDF text layer as tiebreaker, rather than swapping one OCR for another.

## 2026-10-21 12:42–13:45 EDT: a second reading of every equation (side session)

- **Two readers and a referee.** The vision model now reads every equation crop in the background job, and Docling's OCR is only trusted where the two agree. Where they disagree, the PDF's own text layer referees: its glyphs come out scrambled (scripts and fraction parts out of order) but they are what is printed, so comparing bags of symbols is enough. On the Springer flagship that fixed 17 of 32 equations, including the K⁻¹ that the OCR read as K¹ in four places and the three equations it garbled; eq 27 had been "verified" against the wrong LaTeX.
- **Springer's text layer is a cipher.** Its math fonts put = at ¼, / at =, + at þ, brackets at ð Þ ½, the minus at a NUL byte, the prime at 0, ∈ at a lunate epsilon, the radical sign at a lone r, and a radical's bar comes out as "ffiffiffi". TeX's tall delimiters come out as "0 B B @", "1 C C A", "2 6 6 4". Each mapping removed a false disagreement.
- **On clean arXiv PDFs the parser was already right** 18 of 20 times against the source LaTeX; the second reading agreed on 18 and never overrode a correct parse. Most remaining disagreements there were notation (the vision model writes `\pmb`, `\operatorname*{\mathrm{det}}`, wraps lines in braces), which led to three comparator equivalences for things that print the same: a one-symbol function argument in parentheses, where an evaluation bar's or a bracket's exponent attaches, and operator names. They also let 3 more benchmark narrations through the gate (checked by hand: faithful) and moved the headline numbers by 1–3 points.
- **Variance is real.** One flagship equation lost its ✓ only because its context sentence had changed in an earlier rebuild, so its narration was redone and came back with μ for m. Same LaTeX, different wording, different verdict.
- **The built app couldn't open PDFs on this machine:** the Windows registry maps .mjs to text/plain, and Python's mimetypes believes it, so the pdf.js worker module was refused. Invisible in the Vite dev server.
- **Not done:** the narrator/checker prompt fixes ("capital Theta", "end exponent" is not a symbol). They would change every narration and so detach the manual adjudication and the ratings from what the app speaks; left for the user to decide.

## 2026-10-21 14:25–16:45 EDT: prompt fixes, tried and not shipped

- **The judge shared the checker's prompt.** `run.py` used the gate's transcription prompt for the independent judge too, so changing the checker silently changed how every pipeline was scored (even the rules reading moved). The judge's prompt is frozen in `run.py` now; all variants were re-judged with it.
- **More rules made the narrator worse.** Telling `qwen3.5:9b` to say "capital" before uppercase Greek and "evaluated at" for evaluation bars fixed the flagship cases it was written for (eq 14 verified at last), but on the 220-equation benchmark it said "capital" before Latin and calligraphic letters and "evaluated at" for ordinary function arguments: unverified faithfulness fell from 61 to 53 %, gated from 89 to 87 %. Changing only the checker ("end exponent" is not a symbol) made the gate more lenient: 4 more narrations spoken, recall down from 78 to 74 %.

## 2026-10-21 evening: the listening check (with the user)

- **The user's ears found what no metric did.** 30 clips rendered exactly as the app plays them, on a private page with a note per clip; 18 notes came back. Most were about equation wording: a scientist does not say "sub", "open parenthesis" or "d-th power", says "where" for the comma before a condition, "times" after a fraction, "the concentration of A" for [A] and "the derivative of … with respect to time" for d[A]/dt. Long equations (35 s and 106 s) were "way too long to keep following".
- **Exact and natural are different texts.** The gate needs the explicit wording to check a narration; a listener wants less. So the checked text stays explicit (Precise mode, what the benchmark scores) and the app speaks a deterministic rewrite of it by default. Auto mode replaces readings over 50 words with the labeled AI summary.
- **The voice's phonemizer decides small things.** A lone "a" is the article (panel "Figure 1 a" became "uh"), an all-caps "SMILES" is spelled out, "N-vinylcarbazole" gets a short i while "N vinyl carbazole" gets the dictionary's long one. Checking misaki's phonemes before asking the user made most fixes one try.
- **Some bugs were visible without listening:** citation ranges with a baseline dash ("12-16" read as "to") and markers the sentence splitter moved to the next sentence ("19 Another…"). Round 2 gives the user Before/After pairs and two A/B picks (a weak final R; "multi-fidelity").

- **The install path works.** A fresh clone of the private repo plus `setup.ps1` finished in 50 s with warm caches, built UI assets identical to the main checkout's, and `dev.ps1 -NoBrowser -Port 8766` served a working app: the pdf.js worker loaded (the `.mjs` fix holds), the flagship parsed, 32 badges appeared, a clicked sentence played and moved on, and the five equation modes are in the menu. 390 tests passed before this block's changes.
- **Another app on the GPU makes everything AI look broken.** Another app was holding the GPU at 100 %: the cold parse took 5 min 22 s instead of ~100 s, the second reading crawled, and Explain timed out after 300 s with "Ollama is not reachable" (it was reachable, just starved: 1K of 20K prompt tokens in 3 min). - **A fresh install reproduces the checked results exactly.** On the clean clone's first open, the background checks took 7 min 20 s, mostly because the second reading and the figure descriptions both run on `qwen3.5:9b` at once (44 vision calls, ~7 s each, two at a time; the "~2 s per equation" in NOTES was measured without the figure job). - **The citation rule was eating exponents.** Listing every superscript run dropped as a citation marker across the 7 parsed papers showed R² read as "R" (8 times in the GNN paper, the paper's main metric), σ² as "sigma", 10⁷ as "10" (a whole sentence of "51 for 10 hertz, 77 for 10 hertz…"), mW/cm² as "per centimeter" (10 MDPI sentences) and f_{n+1} as "f n+". A raised run is an exponent when it follows a lone symbol, a bare 10, a unit (2 or 3) or a glued minus; digits after "+", "(" or before a glued letter are indices read as printed. The normalizer already reads Unicode superscripts in context, so integer exponents are rewritten as superscript characters (same length, so the word map holds) and only decimals (10^1.78) get words. 34 sentences in 5 papers changed, all for the better; no citation started being read.
- **Springer encodes symbols, not just math.** Their prime is a "0" glyph a third as wide as a digit (1.4 pt at 8.7 pt), and ∈ is U+03F5; both are now fixed in prose for Springer-encoded papers, which changes exactly the two flagship sentences that had them.
- **Docling merges running headers into text.** On MDPI pages where a paragraph's box starts above the header, the page number ("5 of 24") became the paragraph's first words, and figures pasted from the review version carry their own header line. Dropping them from speech (instead of cutting the text) kept every sentence id, so reading positions and caches stay valid.

## 2026-10-21 23:15 – 10-22 00:35 EDT: figure narration and movable popups (side session)

And narration only fired at a target's first mention in the whole paper: start reading after it, and that figure was skipped for the session, caption included, since it counted as "mentioned".

As floating panels over the area, one clamp keeps them clear of the top bar, side panels, scrollbar and playback bar wherever they are dragged. ## 2026-10-22 00:40–03:50 EDT: numbering from the page, and a pronunciation dataset per user (side session)

- **Docling's caption links can be the wrong neighbour.** On MDPI pages the parser linked "Figure 7." to the figure below it and, on one page, every caption to the figure below; other captions came out as paragraphs, and PA12's sat behind a printout's header line. Papers put captions on one side of their figures, so each labeled caption now takes the nearest figure on that side, judged from Docling's own (mostly right) links with a strong prior for "above". Checked offline on all 8 papers before anything was rebuilt: no number changed on the four papers that were right, MDPI went from 11 to 20 numbered figures and PA12 from 8 to 11.
- **The text layer had the equation numbers all along.** IOP's equations end with "(6)" or "(1a,b)" in the PDF's characters; only Docling's copy lost the parentheses. Reading the number at the line's right end gave 30 of 31 at first: equation 10 failed a "is it a raised script?" size test because tall delimiters made its median character large. Comparing the number's largest character instead fixed it, and the glued-to-the-formula test still catches real scripts like h^(1).
- **Rebuilding is safe when ids are the contract.** All 8 papers were rebuilt from their cached Docling parse with a script that refuses to write if any block id, sentence id or sentence text changes; only spoken intros ("Equation 6:") and caption furniture changed, so reading positions, audio and equation checks stayed valid. Cached figure descriptions now carry the figure they describe, so renumbering can't make a popup describe another figure.
- **One dataset file per user.** The user's design: a personal pronunciation dataset that starts as the SciReader list, grows with every pronunciation they give, and moves between users as one file in one format. The base list flows in on updates without overwriting the user's choices; an import keeps differing pronunciations as a second entry, marked, until the user chooses. Reading never writes the file, so tests and the speech path can't create it as a side effect.
- **Benchmarks must not read a user's dataset.** The units benchmarks called the speech pipeline with its default lexicon, which would now be the user's growing dataset; they use the SciReader list explicitly.
